02 Building the Robot

We built and tested each subsystem on its own first, then brought them together on our 3D-printed chassis. Testing every part separately made it much easier to find problems before running the full line-following program.

Prototyping Parts

1A

Potentiometers

Four potentiometers on a breadboard set Speed, P, I and D. Each one works as a voltage divider read by the ESP32's ADC, so we could retune the robot right at the track without uploading new code.

1B

Photoresistors

Seven photoresistors, each in a voltage divider with a 10 kΩ resistor and wired to IO1–IO7, make up the line sensor. Black tape reflects less light than white paper, so the voltage at each sensor shows whether it is over the line.

1C

Motor Driver & ESP32

An L298N dual H-bridge drives the two DC gear motors. IN1–IN4 set each motor's direction and ENA/ENB take PWM signals for speed. We soldered leads onto the motor tabs and covered the joints in hot glue for strain relief. All grounds share a common connection.

1D

CAD the Chassis

We started from the provided chassis in Onshape and changed it to fit our robot. The motors mount at the rear and a ball caster supports the front.

Assembly

  1. Taped the yellow gear motors to the chassis and screwed the ball caster on with M3×6 screws.
  2. Mounted the ESP32, the L298N and the breadboards, and organized the wiring with tape.
  3. Mounted the photoresistor array at the front at a fixed height, with a light shield around it.
  4. Re-ran each test program (potentiometers, photoresistors, motors) on the assembled robot before running the full code.
  5. Added an indicator LED so we could see which calibration step the robot was on.
